BACKGROUND: EXAMPLE 1

In case you need it, here are some general example discussion points. If you don't need these, feel free to skip ahead.

General AI Ethics and Principles

Understanding the ethical foundations of technology requires blending traditional moral frameworks with localised and contextual perspectives. This track explores core philosophical concepts, like autonomy and distributive justice and African ethics centered on Ubuntu. It emphasizes the importance of community, participatory AI design, and the critical need for informed consent within the broader ecosystem of AI development.

Group Prompts:

  • Balancing Frameworks: How do foundational concepts like consequentialism or rights-based ethics intersect with the community-driven focus of Ubuntu?
  • Grassroots Innovation: What practical lessons about inclusive development can be drawn from participatory AI initiatives like Masakhane, SisonkeBiotik, and the Deep Learning Indaba?
  • Ecosystem Impact: In the current AI ecology, what steps are necessary to ensure that principles of distributive justice are upheld for all communities?
  • Navigating Consent: What are the most significant barriers to achieving genuine informed consent and protecting privacy in modern AI applications?

BACKGROUND: EXAMPLE 2

In case you need it, here is another example discussion topic. If you don't need these, feel free to skip ahead.

Artificial Intelligence: Bias, Privacy, and Socio-Economic Impact

As AI systems become deeply integrated into society, they present significant socio-economic and ethical challenges. This track examines the impact of AI on employment, particularly concerning informal and small businesses. It highlights the critical issues of data and annotator bias, especially their effects on African populations alongside algorithmic fairness. Finally, it explores the ongoing challenge of protecting individual privacy while mitigating the risks of de-anonymisation.

Group Prompts:

  • Employment Displacement: How can policies and frameworks be adapted to mitigate the impact of AI on job markets, particularly for informal sectors and small businesses?
  • Tackling Bias: What practical strategies can be implemented to identify and address both annotator bias and data bias in relation to African populations?
  • Algorithmic Fairness: How can we actively detect and mitigate algorithmic biases to ensure AI systems do not perpetuate existing systemic inequalities?
  • Privacy and Anonymisation: In the age of big data, what are the ethical risks posed by deanonymisation, and how can we better implement effective privacy-preserving techniques?

BACKGROUND: OTHER TOPICS

If the previous examples don't fit your expertise, you can apply this discussion framework to your own context. Below are some other crucial areas of Responsible AI that you might consider:

  • Statistical Foundations of Data Science: P-hacking, Spurious Correlations, Proxies of Poverty
  • Data Visualisation and Exploration: Interpretive Divergence, Deceptive Practices, Epistemic Injustice
  • Computer Vision: Privacy and Surveillance, De-identification, Explainability, Intellectual Property, Bias in Health Care
  • Natural Language Processing: Bias in Language, Data Sharing and Curation, Low Resource Languages
  • High-Performance Computing: Large Models vs. Low-Resource Communities, Carbon Emissions, Ethical Data Scraping
  • Machine Learning General: Mathematical/Algorithmic Bias, Fairness, Privacy-preserving ML
  • Explainability: Transparency, Interpretability, Accountability
  • Robotics: Accessibility and Human-Computer Interaction, Job Displacement, Accountability
  • Reinforcement Learning: Alignment, Reward Hacking

ACTIVITY 1

This is where it all starts. The first thing that you need to do is simple. Find yourself a group of 3-4 people and interview each other using the cue card on the next page.

ACTIVITY 1 - CUE CARD

  • Question 1: What is your name and where are you from?
  • Question 2: Why are you here?
  • Question 3: What are you working on at the moment?

When you are finished, you can pat yourselves on the back, chatter, and wait for the next activity to start.

ACTIVITY 2

In this part of the workshop, you'll need to put on your debate face. Return to your group and interview each other using the cue card on the next page.

ACTIVITY 2 - CUE CARD

  • Question 1: Based on your own experience in your field, what is one really important responsible AI and computing challenge that you can think of?
  • Question 2: Do you think this is especially different or important in the South African or African context?
  • Question 3: What do you think is the correct answer or best solution to this?
  • Question 4: What does everyone else in the group think?
